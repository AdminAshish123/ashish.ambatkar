Why Slow Mornings Make Better Days

There is a quiet kind of power in starting the day slowly. Most advice about productivity pushes you to move fast the moment you wake up — check your phone, jump into tasks, race the clock. But a slower start often leads to clearer thinking and steadier decisions for the rest of the day.

When you give yourself even fifteen unhurried minutes before diving into obligations, your mind has room to settle. You notice what you actually want to focus on, instead of reacting to whatever demands your attention first. That small pause compounds into calmer, more intentional choices later on.

This is not about waking up early or following a rigid routine. It is about protecting a small window of stillness, however you find it, before the noise of the day takes over.
